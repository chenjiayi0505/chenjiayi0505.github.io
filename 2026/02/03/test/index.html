ii). Impact of Screen Area

Given the screen’s diagonal length $D$ (inches; 1 inch $= 2.54 \mathrm{~cm}$ ) and aspect ratio $a: b$ , the effective display area is calculated as:

$$S = (D \times 2.54)^2 \times \frac{a \times b}{a^2 + b^2}$$

iii).Impact of Resolution

ii). Impact of ResolutionAccording to physical principles, the total screen power consumption $P_{total}$ (including driver power consumption and luminescence power consumption) satisfies:

$$P_{total}\propto \sqrt{R_{es}}$$

This is jointly determined by three core physical principles: the matrix scanning mechanism of the display driver circuit, the energy consumption law of capacitor charging and discharging, and the visual marginal effect of pixel luminescence. Among them, the load characteristics of the driver circuit are the fundamental cause, and the luminescence characteristics serve as a corrective factor.When substituting the square- root law of driver power consumption into the total screen power consumption model, and taking 1080P as the reference resolution $R_{es_0}$ ,the power consumption correction coefficient for the target resolution $R_{es}$ is:

$$k_{Res} = \sqrt{\frac{R_{es_0}}{R_{es}}} = \sqrt{\frac{1080P}{R_{es}}}$$

The reference power values are adjusted to the target device’s power using the resolution coefficient $k_{Res}$ :

$$P_{s,\max} = P_{s,\max 0}\times k_{Res}$$

b. Impact of Processor Load

The core of the CPU is the CMOS (Complementary Metal- Oxide- Semiconductor) circuit. Its energy consumption essence is “dynamic switching power + static leakage power.” Among these, dynamic switching power accounts for $70% - 90%$ of total power consumption (approaching $100%$ under high load) and strictly follows the laws of semiconductor physics — this is the fundamental reason why the load term has a quadratic relationship.

The basic unit of a CMOS circuit is an inverter (composed of a PMOS and an NMOS transistor). When the input signal changes, the transistors alternately turn on/off, charging and discharging the load capacitor $C_L$ . The energy consumed in this process is the dynamic power:

  • Energy consumed per charge/discharge cycle: $E_{\mathrm{switch}} = C_L \cdot V_{DD}^2$ (where $V_{DD}$ is the supply voltage, a hardware inherent parameter);- Number of switching events per unit time (switching frequency): $f_{\mathrm{switch}} = f_{\mathrm{CPU}} \cdot L(t)$ (where $f_{\mathrm{CPU}}$ is the CPU clock frequency, $L(t)$ is the load percentage);- Physical logic: Load $L(t)$ essentially represents “the percentage of instructions requiring computation per unit time.” Higher load means more frequent transistor switching; $f_{\mathrm{switch}}$ is proportional to $L(t)$ .

Dynamic power formula :

$$P_{\mathrm{dynamic}} = E_{\mathrm{switch}} \cdot f_{\mathrm{switch}} = C_L \cdot V_{DD}^2 \cdot f_{\mathrm{CPU}} \cdot L(t)$$
